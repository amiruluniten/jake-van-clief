# What Jake thinks (by topic)

Each point links to the public post, video or page it comes from and ends with the date of its newest supporting quote. Where his position is contested or has shifted, both sides are kept, each dated.

## Agents vs. folders (ICM)
- Most "agents" aren't worth building. One capable model plus one good harness pointed at a well-structured folder of markdown does the job. "Agents are a waste of time." ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2086277229986648551)) "Why not skip the agent entirely and instead make the folder the focus." ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2089434820187439540)) "The folder is the agent." ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/agentless)) (2026)
- An "agent" is mostly a framing, not a thing: "An Agent is a UX and UI design feature not a product." ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2088665126379389170)) In deployed systems it's "a collection of prompts structured and sequenced by traditional code" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGyTimVObus)), and "language is running ahead of the engineering." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGyTimVObus)) (2026)
- Flip the architecture: "1 Agent sent to 100 contexts = Right" ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2083554499000406238)). Context structure beats model cleverness: "Context structure is the only thing that matters" ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2086277034632704107)), "It's all about data structure" ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2074947374707826730)). (2026)
- ICM's core: numbered folders are stages, CONTEXT.md files are stage contracts, one agent "reads the right files at the right moment" ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology)). Principles he repeats: configure the factory, not the product ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology)); one canonical home per rule, because "The moment the same rule exists in two files, they drift." ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology)); "Early outputs are the worst outputs," so agents learn from docs, not old outputs ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology)); separate your work so the model isn't reading everything ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MkN-ss2Nl10)). (2026)
- It's glass-box by default: "It was never opaque in the first place, because every artifact is a plain-text file that a human can read." ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology)) (2026)
- Markdown isn't a database: "The files are instructions… The file is the routing layer. The database is still the database." ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2032438202896708068)) (2026)
- Where this is heading: "all just going to end up at Unix methodology" ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2114096799979909433)); "going backward before going forward." ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology)) Claude Design and skills are "literally just an ICM" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YETgVWRHilw)). (2026)
- Agents are "just system prompts trapped inside code" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dZ8P7zevuGw)); with folders "The folders become the architecture" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPJ0gbCUSQk)) and you're not locked to one vendor: "I can use any AI." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dZ8P7zevuGw)) Second brains and company OSes are "just really well organized folder structures, markdown files." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FYLbILHbJgI)) (2026)
- Raw models "actually suck… in the most endearing way possible". What makes them useful is context: prompts, tools and data in the right order ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPJ0gbCUSQk)). Swarms: "Complete waste of time… Way too much structure." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPJ0gbCUSQk)) (2026)
- Plain files mean resilience: "If AI breaks down tomorrow, we still have a whole human-readable process." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NWyTsKTKka8)) (2026)
- **Contested / nuance (keep both):** "there might be 1% of the time where it's actually useful" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPJ0gbCUSQk), 2026-10), and in the 2026 webinar he calls swarms, OpenClaw etc. "people's bespoke solutions in the same way ICM's my own solution for orchestration." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NWyTsKTKka8), 2026-08) On X it's absolute ("Agents are a waste of time", [X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2086277229986648551), 2026-08). In the ICM README he's measured: frameworks are "genuinely good… They work" ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology), 2026) and "ICM is not a replacement for multi-agent frameworks in every context" ([GitHub](https://github.com/RinDig/Interpretable-Context-Methodology), 2026): real-time multi-agent loops, high concurrency and automated branching are where it breaks. He also admits "there are situations where you do" need extra infrastructure, so his team built a deployment layer ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2114701228722569510), 2026-10). The October 2026 lines (the 1% exception and the deployment-layer admission) are newer than the August 2026 X post and webinar; the README is dated only to 2026, so it isn't pinned to a month.

## Frameworks, hype and "the AI inside"
- Bespoke agent engineering ignores the Bitter Lesson ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2089460008287420798)). Burning tokens isn't progress: "1 billion tokens spent over night. Still no product market fit" ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2115099714479669261)). (2026)
- Open the box before you believe the hype. Moltbot "is generous branding for what's essentially a message router. The hype has outpaced the understanding." ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2017044759290773740)) "The AI is a component, not the whole thing." ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2017044641590169660)) (2026)
- There is "a massive AI bubble" like the early internet, and he still loves the tech ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RZ0AcCLVPFA)): "the bubble will burst. AI will crash. But from that crash, value will be pulled from just like the dot crash" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AjzBaEkWkNA)). AI doomerism is "Lobbying" ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2099728598428983807)). (2026)
- Use AI last, not first. Companies jump "on AI to solve a problem that a simple SQL call or a Python script could solve" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AjzBaEkWkNA), 2025). The mix should be mostly traditional software: "60% traditional software, 20% rule-based logic… and then 10% 20% AI" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AjzBaEkWkNA), 2025). His LinkedIn version is 60/30/10 ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/in/jake-van-clief), date unknown). The exact split varies, the direction doesn't. The LinkedIn wording has no date in the ledger or raw cache, so it isn't clear which split is newer.
- We're in a Solow-paradox moment: "We see the AI age everywhere but in the productivity statistics." Productivity comes once workflows are redesigned around augmentation ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NWyTsKTKka8)). Engelbart: "The human sits at the center. The tools extend the reach." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Ds2ZJD37KY)) (2026)

## What AI is: programs, compilers, abstraction
- "AI is part of the program, it is not a program." ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2112713189443907823)) A program = instructions + something that runs them + state that survives ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/ai-is-part-of-a-program-it-is-not)). AI's power is "a new layer in our ability to run instructions," not replacing work ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/ai-is-part-of-a-program-it-is-not)). (2026)
- Models got useful because the instructions got better, not because they got smarter ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/ai-is-part-of-a-program-it-is-not)). They're "already smart. Or at least smart enough." ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/the-machine-is-smart)) (2026)
- AI is the next compiler: "a compiler for human intent" ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/why-even-y-combinator-cant-crack)). If you feel behind, "you're not operating on fundamental truths" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RZ0AcCLVPFA)). (2025)
- Intent comes from dialogue: "Conversation has the structure we're looking for." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=956DPSPX4wg)) (date unknown)

## Taste, opinion and "best"
- "Best" is relative to context, which is "the whole game" ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/the-machine-is-smart)). The model has read everything about your job but doesn't know your version of best, so "productionize your opinion not just your process." ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/the-machine-is-smart)) (He also uses a Pagani-vs-Toyota car analogy for this; that recording is multi-speaker, [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-YursW-UIoY), date unknown). (2026)
- Software should fit people, not the reverse (Levine the Tailor; Weinberg): "the best software is one that fits to us" ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/ai-is-part-of-a-program-it-is-not), [X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2097887761680494948)). Programming is human: "closer to poetry than it is to math" ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/ai-is-part-of-a-program-it-is-not)); "Programing is psychology" ([X](https://x.com/VanCliefMedia/status/2116740837652447539)). (2026)
- Take other people's non-opinionated building blocks; own the opinionated "why" ([Substack](https://jakevanclief.substack.com/p/ai-is-part-of-a-program-it-is-not)). (2026)
- What to study for the next decade: Unix philosophy, Engelbart's 1962 paper, traditional software logic ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JPJ0gbCUSQk)). (2026)
